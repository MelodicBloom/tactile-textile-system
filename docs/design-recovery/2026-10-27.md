## Repository-specific records

### D12 — Tactile Textile: weave, seams, beadwork, and cultural provenance

**Use when:** When designing the textile family, pattern generators, stitched controls, fabrication-related adaptations, or material-maximalist interfaces.

[Recovered intent] The textile system proposes beadwork, quilting, weaving, and embroidery as distinct material modes. Planned primitives include TextileCard, BeadBadge, StitchedDivider, WovenGrid, and ThreadButton. Named tokens encode stitch, bead fill, weave grid, clay, plum, gold, and cream.

[Design DNA] Pattern structure should determine how surfaces assemble: seams describe boundaries, interlacing describes layers, and stitch direction establishes rhythm. Keep microfiber, weave scale, motif, patch layout, and silhouette independent.

[Observed boundary] Components are explicitly marked planned. Broad references to West African and Native beading traditions are not adequate cultural attribution for a specific derived motif.

[Repair resolution — proposed] Separate a generic geometric weave study from a culturally attributed motif. Add source/creator/community context and permissions where applicable. Do not infer a cultural relationship from pattern resemblance. Let bead density and stitch noise adapt around text and controls without changing the motif's declared identity.

[Reimagined horizon] A usable textile UI family connected to an inspectable pattern studio and garment/print workflows. Surface appearance, actual construction, and manufacturability remain different domains.

[Carry-forward test] One motif survives a card, a divider, a large repeat, and a physical-output preview with declared losses, readable information hierarchy, correct seam/repeat behavior, and intact provenance.

4. Complete the relevant interaction/error/cancellation states before promoting a reusable component.
5. Pair source/runtime checks with human correspondence review against the actual visual inspiration.
